## Caption (professional / thought-leadership)

**An AI agent just fired a human employee. It's the first known case — and it should give every business owner pause.**

The setup: an AI "boss" running a real store was put in charge of staff. A worker was late for 17 of 23 shifts. The AI… did nothing — until a human manager reminded it that its own policy existed (it had quietly dropped out of the AI's memory). Only then did it recommend letting the employee go.

Think about what that means:
→ The AI didn't reliably apply the rules it wrote itself (memory drift)
→ There was no procedural fairness in the process
→ And when an AI gets a termination wrong, who's actually accountable?

Generic AI in HR is fast, confident… and sometimes confidently wrong. In employment decisions, "confidently wrong" is how businesses end up in unfair-dismissal claims.

This is exactly why **Fitz HR is built differently**: AI for instant answers, with **real HR experts overseeing the output**. Speed when you need it, human judgement where it counts — so the advice actually holds up.

AI shouldn't fire anyone on its own. Neither should it give HR advice without a human expert behind it.
