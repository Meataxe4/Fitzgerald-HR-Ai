Hi [first name],

[One personalised line — reference their recent relevant piece. Examples below.]

The 2026 Annual Wage Review rates took effect 1 July — a 4.75% rise across the modern
awards — and the errors employers make in the first payroll cycles are the expensive kind:
Fair Work penalties for award non-compliance run up to $93,900 per contravention for
companies, and a typical unfair dismissal claim costs $10,000–$50,000+ once legal fees land.

Three mistakes I can walk your readers through, with current figures:

1. **Applying the new base rate but missing the flat-dollar loadings.** Under the
   Hospitality Award, evening work (7pm–midnight) adds a flat $2.95/hr and night work
   $4.42/hr — flat dollars, not percentages. Applying them as a percentage of the new
   base is a systematic underpayment.
2. **Stacking the 25% casual loading on penalty rates that already include it.** A
   hospitality casual on Sunday is paid 175% of the ordinary rate — the loading is
   already in that number. Adding 25% on top is an overpayment; assuming the reverse
   is an underpayment.
3. **Assuming every award moved the same way.** From 1 July the minimum full-time
   adult rate starts at $26.44/hr under Hospitality, $27.81 under Retail, $27.55 under
   SCHADS and $25.74 at C14 under Manufacturing — and each award's penalties, allowances
   and loadings shifted on its own schedule. A SCHADS sleepover is not a hospitality
   split shift.

I'm the founder of Fitz HR (fitzhr.com), an Australian platform that maintains verified
rate data across seven modern awards, updated against the Fair Work Ombudsman Pay Guides
each Annual Wage Review. Happy to supply exact before/after tables for your readers'
sector, or a short expert comment — same-day turnaround.

Blake Fitzgerald
Founder, Fitz HR · fitzhr.com · support@fitzhr.com

- Skew: emphasise process risk — "most claims are lost on documentation, not the decision"

  Sunday 150%/175%; public holidays 225%/250%; evening $2.95/hr and night $4.42/hr flat

### The Sector / childcare press
- Subject: "New Children's Services Award rates: the 3 mistakes services are making this July"

  qualification-based classifications, and the educational leader allowance

  allowance right now — a story their readers are actively googling
