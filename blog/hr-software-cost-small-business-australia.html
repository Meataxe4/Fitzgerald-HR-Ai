What HR Software Really Costs a 15-Person Team in Australia

27 Jul 2026 By Fitz HR 6 min read

Every HR platform advertises a small number — $4.50 here, $8 there, $25 per user. None of them advertise what that means for your team over a year. This guide runs the arithmetic for a typical 15-person Australian small business using published prices, and explains why the pricing model matters more than the sticker.

Quick answer

In 2026, per-employee HR platforms list between roughly A$4.50 and A$25 per user per month — about $810 to $4,500 a year for a 15-person team, before add-ons. Flat-priced software like Fitz HR runs $0–$899 a year regardless of headcount ($249–$449/year covers most small businesses). The bigger the share of casuals on your books, the more per-user pricing penalises you — it bills heads, not hours.

The Two Pricing Models

Per employee per month is the dominant model: Employment Hero lists from around $8 per employee/month, Deputy (a rostering tool, not HR) from around $4.50 per user/month, and Sentrient’s HR Management System is listed on Capterra at around $25 per user/month (all July 2026 published/listed prices). The number looks small because it’s quoted per head — the annual bill is the number times your headcount times twelve.

Flat annual pricing charges for the product, not the headcount. Fitz HR’s published plans are Free ($0), Starter ($249/year), Pro ($449/year) and Business ($899/year) — the same price whether you employ 4 staff or 40.

The Casual-Headcount Trap

Per-user billing has a structural bias against exactly the businesses that need compliance help most. Award-covered industries — hospitality, retail, community services and NDIS, childcare — run casual-heavy rosters. A cafe with 15 staff on the books might roster the equivalent of six full-timers, but per-user software bills all fifteen, including the uni student who works Sunday brunch.

Flat pricing inverts that: the more casual and shift-based your workforce, the better the deal — and casual, shift-based workforces are where award mistakes (wrong penalty rates, stacked loadings, missed casual conversion) actually happen.

Price Is Only Half the Question — Fit Is the Other

The platforms above do different jobs, and the honest comparison says so:

  • Rostering & time tools (Deputy, Tanda, RosterElf) schedule shifts and feed payroll. They don’t answer HR questions or produce defensible documents.
  • HR lifecycle platforms (Employment Hero, Sentrient) handle onboarding, reviews, training records and policies across all industries. Award-specific pay guidance is not their core job — see our full Employment Hero and Sentrient comparisons.
  • Award-grounded HR (Fitz HR) answers the money-and-process questions — what to pay under your award, how to manage a warning or dismissal, which document to issue — from the award itself, verified against the Fair Work Ombudsman Pay Guides.
The Benchmark That Matters

Whatever you pay for HR software, compare it to the cost of getting one decision wrong: a typical unfair dismissal claim runs $10,000–$50,000+ once legal fees land, and Fair Work penalties for award non-compliance reach $93,900 per contravention for companies. Every option in the table costs less than 5% of one bad outcome — the question is which one prevents your likeliest bad outcome.
