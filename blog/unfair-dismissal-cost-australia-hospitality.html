How Much Does an Unfair Dismissal Claim Actually Cost in Australia?

9 Apr 2026 By Fitz HR Updated 7 Oct 2026 8 min read Legally reviewed — 2026

Most small business owners only realise the cost of unfair dismissal after the claim is lodged. By that point, the outcome isn’t whether you’ll spend money — it’s how much. Here are the real numbers, and what actually drives them.

The total cost of an unfair dismissal claim to an Australian small business typically ranges from $5,000 to $70,000+ depending on whether the matter settles at conciliation or proceeds to a hearing. The maximum compensation is 26 weeks’ pay, capped at $95,050 for claims lodged on or after 1 July 2026 (half the $190,100 high income threshold). Most matters settle at conciliation for $5,000–$20,000. Legal fees are additional and can exceed the compensation itself.

The Compensation Cap — What It Actually Means

Under the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), the maximum compensation the Commission can order for unfair dismissal is the lesser of 26 weeks’ pay and half the high income threshold. The threshold rose to $190,100 on 1 July 2026, so the cap for claims lodged from that date is $95,050, up from $91,550 the year before. It moves every 1 July.

For most award-covered employees the 26-week limb is the one that matters. An employee on $60,000 a year has a 26-week figure of about $30,000; one on $45,000, about $22,500. The $95,050 cap only bites for employees earning above roughly $190,000. Legal fees sit on top of whichever figure applies.

How the Commission arrives at a figure

Compensation is not a fine and it is not damages for hurt feelings. The Commission works through a set sequence:

  • Lost remuneration. What the employee would likely have earned had they not been dismissed, over the period they would probably have remained employed. A worker already on a final warning has a shorter likely period than one with a clean record.
  • Less earnings since dismissal. Anything the employee has earned, or could reasonably have earned, in other work since the dismissal is deducted. This is why the time between dismissal and hearing matters.
  • Contingencies. An adjustment for the ordinary uncertainties of continued employment.
  • Misconduct. If the employee’s own conduct contributed to the dismissal, the figure is reduced.
  • The cap. Finally the lesser of 26 weeks’ pay or $95,050 is applied.

Nothing is added for shock, distress or humiliation. Reinstatement, where the Commission orders you to re-employ the person, is the primary remedy in the Act but is ordered only where it is appropriate; most employers who face it regard it as worse than paying compensation.

Who can bring a claim

Compensation is only a risk if the employee can lodge. They can if they have completed the minimum employment period (six months, or twelve months where the employer has fewer than 15 employees), are covered by a modern award or enterprise agreement or earn under $190,100, and lodge within 21 days of the dismissal taking effect. Regular and systematic casuals with a reasonable expectation of ongoing work count once they pass the minimum period. Dismissal inside the minimum period removes unfair dismissal exposure but not general protections exposure, which has no service requirement. See firing during probation.

What the Process Actually Looks Like

Understanding the timeline helps you understand where costs accumulate:

Claim lodged (Day 0–21 after dismissal). The employee has 21 days from the dismissal date to lodge a claim. Once lodged, you receive a copy and must respond within 7 days. Your legal position is set from this point.
2
Conciliation (Week 6–8). The Commission appoints a conciliator and schedules a confidential telephone conference. Both parties attend. Most matters settle here — this is where your legal fees start and where settlement is negotiated. If it settles, the matter ends. If not, it proceeds to formal hearing.
Formal hearing (Month 3–6+). If conciliation fails, the matter is listed for hearing before a Commissioner. Both sides present evidence and submissions. Legal costs escalate significantly. Outcomes are published in FWC decisions — they become public record.

The decision to fight a claim to hearing versus settling at conciliation is one of the most important cost decisions you’ll make. Most employment lawyers recommend settling unless you have an unusually strong case AND the compensation amount at risk is low.

What Actually Drives Up the Cost

The single biggest cost driver is not the severity of the dismissal — it’s the state of your documentation. Employers with complete paper trails settle faster and at lower amounts. Employers without documentation lose leverage at every stage.

No written warnings on file. Without documented prior warnings, the employer cannot establish that the employee had a genuine opportunity to improve. This turns a defensible termination into a compensation negotiation.
No record of the termination meeting. If there’s no written record of what was said, why, and what the employee’s response was, the Commission must rely on competing accounts from the employer and employee. This is rarely advantageous for the employer.
Procedural errors in the process. Failing to offer a support person, making the decision before hearing the employee’s response, or giving notice that was too short — each of these is a separate procedural failure that increases compensation. See our guide on procedural fairness in Australian employment law.
Fighting to hearing on a weak case. Legal fees from conciliation to hearing can add $15,000–$25,000+ to the total cost. Fighting a case to hearing without strong documentation is rarely cost-effective.

How to Avoid the Claim Entirely

Most unfair dismissal claims are avoidable. The Fair Work Commission’s own data shows the majority of successful claims hinge on procedural failures — not substantive ones. The employer had a valid reason. They just couldn’t prove the process was fair.

The protection is documentation:

  • Formal written warnings with specific conduct described and improvement timeframes stated
  • A genuine opportunity to respond at every stage
  • A support person offered at formal meetings
  • Written confirmation of the termination decision with reasons
  • A complete file that can be produced to the Commission if needed

Frequently Asked Questions

What does an unfair dismissal claim actually cost employers in Australia?
Total costs typically range from $8,000–$25,000 if settled at conciliation, or $15,000–$70,000+ if the matter proceeds to a formal hearing. Compensation is capped at 26 weeks’ pay (maximum $95,050 for claims lodged on or after 1 July 2026). Legal fees and management time are additional and often exceed the compensation itself.
What is the maximum compensation for unfair dismissal in Australia?
The maximum is the lesser of 26 weeks’ pay and half the high income threshold — $95,050 for claims lodged on or after 1 July 2026. This is the compensation component only. Legal costs are additional. For most award-covered employees the 26-week limb is the binding one, which produces a far lower figure than the cap.
Can I settle an unfair dismissal claim before it goes to a hearing?
Yes — the vast majority of unfair dismissal claims settle at conciliation, a confidential mediation process before any formal hearing. Conciliation typically occurs within 6–8 weeks of the claim. Most hospitality employers settle at this stage for $5,000–$20,000 rather than risk a hearing outcome. Settlement terms are confidential.
What is the unfair dismissal compensation cap in 2026?
$95,050 for claims lodged on or after 1 July 2026, which is half the $190,100 high income threshold. The Commission can order at most the lesser of 26 weeks’ pay or that cap. For an employee on $60,000 a year the 26-week figure is about $30,000, so the cap only bites for higher earners.
How does the Fair Work Commission calculate unfair dismissal compensation?
Lost earnings over the likely remaining employment, less earnings since dismissal, adjusted for contingencies and any misconduct, then capped. Nothing is added for shock, distress or humiliation. The cap is the lesser of 26 weeks’ pay or $95,050.
Who can make an unfair dismissal claim?
An employee past the minimum employment period (six months, or twelve for employers with fewer than 15 staff), covered by an award or agreement or earning under $190,100, who lodges within 21 days of the dismissal taking effect. Regular and systematic casuals qualify once past the minimum period. See firing during probation and how many warnings before dismissal.
Does it cost an employee anything to lodge a claim?
The application fee is $92.70 from 1 July 2026, and it can be waived for serious financial hardship. There is no filing fee for the employer, but the employer carries its own legal costs and management time from the day the claim arrives.
How do I avoid an unfair dismissal claim?
Document the process at every stage: formal written warnings, genuine improvement periods, an opportunity to respond, and written confirmation of the termination. Most unfair dismissal findings are procedural — the employer had a valid reason but couldn’t prove the process was fair. Documentation is the protection.
Does unfair dismissal apply to casual employees in hospitality?
Yes — once employed on a regular and systematic basis for at least 6 months (12 months for small businesses). Simply ceasing to roster a long-term casual is treated as a dismissal at the Fair Work Commission. Without a documented fair process, it will almost certainly be found unfair.
