Retail Award Penalty Rates — Sunday & Public Holidays

1 Jul 2026 Updated 1 Jul 2026 By Fitz HR 5 min read Reviewed against FWO Pay Guide MA000004

Weekend and public holiday penalty rates are where retail underpayment claims are won and lost. This guide sets out the exact General Retail Industry Award MA000004 penalty rates for 2026 and how they apply to full-time, part-time and casual employees.

Under MA000004, Saturday is 125% (full-time/part-time) or 150% (casual); Sunday is 150% or 175%; public holidays are 225% or 250%. Casual percentages already include the 25% casual loading. A retail employee who works a public holiday can be offered a substitute day only by agreement, and part-time/casual employees are still owed their minimum 3-hour engagement.

Rates current as at 2026-07-01 (Annual Wage Review), sourced from the Fair Work Ombudsman Pay Guide MA000004. Next review 2027-07-01.

Retail Penalty Rates 2026

Saturday: 125% (FT/PT) · 150% (casual)
Sunday: 150% (FT/PT) · 175% (casual)
Public holiday: 225% (FT/PT) · 250% (casual)
Casual loading: 25%, already included above

The Penalty Rate Table

All percentages are of the ordinary hourly rate. Casual rates are all-inclusive of the 25% loading.

Worked Examples

For a Retail Employee Level 1 on $27.81/hr:

  • Saturday: $34.76/hr (FT/PT) · $41.71/hr (casual)
  • Sunday: $41.71/hr (FT/PT) · $48.67/hr (casual)
  • Public holiday: $62.57/hr (FT/PT) · $69.52/hr (casual)

A single public holiday shift paid at Sunday rates instead of the correct public holiday rate is an underpayment on every hour worked.

Public Holiday Rules

Under the National Employment Standards, an employee can refuse to work a public holiday if the request is unreasonable or their refusal is reasonable. A part-time or casual employee who does work still receives their minimum 3-hour engagement. A substitute day can only replace a public holiday by genuine agreement.

Common Mistakes That Trigger Audits

Paying Sunday rates on public holidays. Public holidays are 225%/250%, not 150%/175%.
Stacking casual loading on the casual penalty rate. The casual percentages already include the 25% loading.
Forgetting the minimum engagement on weekends. Part-time and casual employees are owed at least 3 hours per shift.
