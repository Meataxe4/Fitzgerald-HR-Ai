Fitz HR vs ChatGPT for HR compliance — confident is not the same as correct

What Each Tool Actually Is

ChatGPT is a general-purpose AI assistant. It has read a great deal about Australian employment law and can explain the Fair Work Act, the National Employment Standards and how modern awards work in general terms. It is not connected to your award, your classifications, your roster or the Pay Guide that took effect at the last Annual Wage Review, and it does not know which of the 100-plus modern awards actually covers your business unless you tell it correctly. When it is unsure, it does not usually say so.

Fitz HR is HR compliance software for Australian small business. It is AI too, but a narrow one: every answer, pay rate and document is grounded in one of seven modern awards — Hospitality, Restaurant, General Retail, Manufacturing, SCHADS, Health Professionals or Children’s Services — with rates sourced from the Fair Work Ombudsman Pay Guides, current as at 1 July 2026, and regression-tested before going live. It generates contracts, warning letters, PIPs and termination documents with the right classification and clauses, keeps a compliance record against each employee, and puts qualified HR professionals on call, including after hours. Flat-priced from $0.

Where Generic AI Goes Wrong on Australian HR

None of these are exotic failures. They are the ordinary ways a general model handles a specific legal question.

  • It does not know which award you are on. A cafe can sit under the Restaurant Award or the Hospitality Award, and the late-night bands and minimum engagements differ. Ask a general assistant about “cafe penalty rates” and you may get a blend of both.
  • Its rates can be stale. Award minimums change every 1 July. A model answers from whatever it was trained on, or from whichever web page it happened to find, and rarely tells you the effective date.
  • It cannot see your classification. The right rate depends on the level the employee is actually classified at. A general assistant guesses the level or asks you to, then builds a confident answer on the guess.
  • It sounds equally sure whether it is right or wrong. That is the expensive part. A wrong penalty rate applied for a year is a back-pay claim; a warning letter that skips procedural fairness is an unfair dismissal claim.
  • It leaves no record. When a claim lands, the question is not just what you decided but whether you can show the process you followed. A chat window is not a compliance record.
  • There is no one to escalate to. The walkout, the theft allegation, the support worker who no-shows a 2am shift: at some point the answer needs a qualified HR professional, not a better prompt.
  • Staff details go into a general tool. Pasting names, pay and conduct issues into a consumer AI product raises privacy questions that a purpose-built HR platform is designed around.

Fitz HR is built to close each of those gaps. That is not a claim that AI is unreliable — Fitz is AI. It is a claim that grounding is the whole game. A model that knows your award, the current Pay Guide and the process rules gives a different answer to one that knows employment law in general.

The Question That Decides It

Before you act on any HR answer, ask three things: Which award and clause is this from? What date are these rates effective? Who stands behind this if it is wrong?

ChatGPT can sometimes answer the first, rarely the second, and never the third. Fitz HR answers all three by design, and hands the matter to a human when the situation is beyond a rate lookup.
